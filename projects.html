Mechanical Design - MEng Thesis

Engineering Design of Actuated Sensor Alignment Systems for Subsea Applications

Engineering design of an actuated sensor alignment system for a gravity-moored subsea sensor platform.

+

Overview

This project investigates the engineering design of an actuated sensor alignment system for a gravity-moored subsea platform. The system is intended to compensate for sensor misalignment caused by tidal forces and maintain the required sensor orientation at depths of up to 100 m.

Project objectives

  • Develop an engineering design specification for the alignment system.
  • Investigate suitable actuator configurations.
  • Evaluate appropriate materials and mechanical components.
  • Analyse the structural behaviour of the proposed design.
  • Use engineering analysis, including FEA and CFD where appropriate.

Technical focus

The project combines mechanical design, actuator selection, structural analysis and subsea engineering considerations. Particular attention is being given to the effects of hydrostatic pressure, tidal loading and actuator requirements.

Computational Fluid Dynamics

Computational Fluid Dynamics Projects

CFD studies investigating internal pipe flow, high-speed train aerodynamics and fluid forces using STAR-CCM+. Completed as assessment in university course lead by Professor David Ingram.

+

1. CFD Simulation of Flow Through an Orifice Plate

Designed and simulated an orifice plate flow meter in a 280 mm diameter pipe using STAR-CCM+, with the aim of predicting the pressure differential across the plate under high-pressure nitrogen flow conditions. The design was developed with reference to BS EN ISO 5167, considering plate geometry, diameter ratio and pressure-tapping locations.

Developed a two-dimensional axisymmetric model using the SST k-ω turbulence model. Investigated mesh refinement, near-wall prism layers, turbulence modelling and the inlet length required to establish fully developed flow. Analysed pressure and velocity fields to identify flow separation, recirculation regions and downstream pressure recovery.

Compared the simulated pressure differential against the theoretical prediction from the ISO 5167 correlations. The initial comparison revealed a significant discrepancy, highlighting the importance of mesh quality, boundary conditions and further validation before CFD results can be used for design.

Key technical areas

  • STAR-CCM+ and axisymmetric CFD modelling
  • SST k-ω turbulence modelling
  • Mesh refinement and near-wall treatment
  • ISO 5167 orifice meter design and pressure-drop calculations
  • Critical evaluation of numerical results against theory

2. CFD Simulation of a High-Speed Shinkansen in the Channel Tunnel

Investigated the aerodynamic effects of operating an E5/H5-class Shinkansen at 313 km/h in the Channel Tunnel, significantly above the existing 160 km/h speed limit considered in the study. Using STAR-CCM+, modelled the interaction between the train and tunnel to assess pressure changes, aerodynamic forces and the flow structures generated around the train.

Developed a three-dimensional model of a four-carriage train within the tunnel, using a simplified geometry to balance physical detail and computational cost. Applied a compressible ideal-gas model and SST k-ω turbulence modelling, with local mesh refinement around the train nose, tail and carriage gaps. Conducted a grid-convergence study using the train drag coefficient and compared steady and unsteady solutions.

The simulations identified accelerated flow between the train and tunnel walls, substantial wake regions and pressure variations along the train. The grid study showed a consistent convergence trend, but numerical uncertainty remained because computational resources limited further refinement. This provided an opportunity to assess the limitations of the model when interpreting engineering loads.

Key technical areas

  • Three-dimensional external and internal flow modelling
  • Compressible flow and ideal-gas modelling
  • Mesh refinement and grid-convergence analysis
  • Boundary-layer and prism-layer meshing
  • Aerodynamic drag, lift and tunnel-wall loading
  • Evaluation of numerical uncertainty and computational constraints
